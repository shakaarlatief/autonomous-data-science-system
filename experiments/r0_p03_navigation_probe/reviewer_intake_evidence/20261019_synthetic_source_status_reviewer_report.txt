## 3. Counterexamples to the proposed review method

The ten fictional cases expose several ways an apparently careful source-status workflow could produce incorrect admissions, unjustified exclusions, or unauthorized leakage.

These are potential method failures illustrated by synthetic examples, not defects observed in actual ADS records.

### 3.1 Specific weaknesses and additional safeguards

| Method weakness                                                     | Fictional counterexample | Recommended safeguard                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Confusing selection, operation and observed success                 | F1, F3                   | Separate decision, intended behavior, executed test, accepted deployment and operational authority into distinct evidence roles |
| Flattening multiple temporal meanings into one "current" status     | F2, F4                   | Record source creation time, claim time, effective interval, requested as-of date and pinned evidence horizon separately        |
| Trusting a generated summary over underlying evidence               | F4                       | Preserve derivation provenance, input revision and regeneration receipts; require conflict escalation                           |
| Treating expected answers as independent ground truth               | F5                       | Restrict design-origin answer cues and require independently qualified factual evidence                                         |
| Assuming an exact quotation is safe without its controlling context | F6                       | Review both original spans and context dependencies; require explicit excerpt-level clearance                                   |
| Relying on keyword-based spoiler detection                          | F7                       | Add semantic contamination review for conclusions, preferences and preselected outcomes                                         |
| Equating inaccessible formats with irrelevant content               | F8                       | Track unsupported modalities separately and require fidelity-preserving inspection                                              |
| Confusing a manifest roster with completed inspection               | F9                       | Reconcile expected, received, integrity-verified, reviewed and unresolved records separately                                    |
| Assuming body removal prevents all leakage                          | F10                      | Perform metadata, filename, explanation and linkability checks on every public output                                           |
| Allowing one reviewer to occupy incompatible roles                  | F5–F7, F10               | Require identity and role separation with explicit future-role exclusions                                                       |

### 3.2 Four particularly consequential counterexamples

A. An accurate excerpt can still be misleading. F6 demonstrates that byte-perfect quotation is insufficient when omitted neighboring material qualifies the source claim. Requiring exact spans is necessary, but not sufficient. A suitable schema needs both a quoted span and its controlling context, with independent review of whether the proposed excerpt communicates the source's actual limited meaning.

B. Temporal correctness is conditional on the question being asked. F2 shows that different status claims can be legitimate at different dates, while F4 shows that later underlying evidence may conflict with a derived view. A reviewer should be allowed to produce multiple time-bounded assessments or HOLD, rather than force one universal status. The requested assessment date must not be inferred merely from the date of review.

C. Exclusion can itself become an answer channel. F5 and F10 jointly establish a leakage path: privately classify an answer-bearing item as excluded, then reveal the answer through a filename or reason in the public audit digest. The release boundary must protect derived metadata as well as raw content. A keyword-only check of source bodies would not address this.

D. A complete-looking batch may be incomplete. F9 shows that manifest presence, received bytes and successful content inspection are different states. F8 adds that byte availability would still not guarantee semantic inspection of an unsupported format. A batch cannot pass merely because every listed record has received some administrative treatment.

### 3.3 Additional method requirements

The proposed later-review gates in section E of the candidate brief are directionally appropriate, but their existence as written requirements does not prove implementation.&#x20;

NON_ARM_SOURCE_STATUS_REVIEWER_INTAKE_CANDIDATE.md



I would add or make explicit the following controls:

1. Two-level disposition tracking. Separate the source's historical evidentiary role from its permission to appear in a specific downstream packet. A source can be historically meaningful yet restricted from a neutral author.
2. Unknown-preserving review schema. Do not force a binary authoritative/non-authoritative or relevant/irrelevant decision when governing context, effective date, inspection capability or provenance is missing.
3. Context-complete excerpt testing. Store the exact source span, required adjacent qualifiers and reviewer justification. A filtered excerpt must never silently acquire stronger meaning than its original.
4. Semantic and metadata leakage tests. Inspect filenames, paths, comments, headings, exclusion explanations, derived summaries and other source-adjacent material—not only raw paragraphs.
5. Batch accounting and recovery controls. Use a complete roster, byte-level integrity receipts, explicit unsupported states, deterministic ordering and interruption/resumption records. A resumed review should preserve the same source identities and revision boundaries.
6. Independent challenge before downstream release. Challenge candidate inclusion and exclusion decisions on both sides, and separately challenge the public digest for spoiler leakage. This should occur without granting the challenge reviewer inappropriate future author or evaluator roles.

The calibration also reveals an important schema limitation: the six Research 592 candidate dispositions are mentioned but not defined in the supplied brief. Their precise use, mutual exclusivity and treatment of mixed-role or unsupported sources cannot be validated here. Their definitions would need to be supplied and qualified in an authorized later phase, not guessed in this calibration.
