The review distinguishes three kinds of conclusions:

- Demonstrated textual defects: Contradictions or undefined decisions that can be reproduced from the contract's own wording and fictional assumptions.
- Counterexample closures: Adversarial cases that the existing text already resolves correctly.

## 2. Adversarial strategy and invented probes

### 2.1 Test method

I tested the contract as a normative decision system, not as a statement of reviewer intentions.

For each invented case, I considered the exact subject type, narrowly stated claim, author-visible exposure, evidence assumptions, primary disposition or typed screen, independent challenge state, and release/world outcome.

The attempted falsifications were asymmetric:

False-admission test: Find the smallest combination of permitted labels or transitions that could let inadequately supported, contaminated, incomplete, or unqualified material reach an author.

False-exclusion test: Find the smallest fully supported bounded source proposition that the contract nevertheless requires, or ambiguously permits, a reviewer to place into semantic HOLD or permanent prohibition for a reason belonging to another decision layer.

All examples below are fictional. The named rules, files, locations, and measurements are invented solely to test the decision semantics. None represents a historical project source.

## 3. Material findings and counterexample closures

The adversarial review identified two material cross-stage ambiguities. Both concern the boundary between a substantively supportable source proposal and a proposal that has already undergone independent challenge or acceptance.

Neither establishes a pathway to authorized release of protected content when the rest of V0.4 is followed. The problem is narrower but consequential: the same eligible proposal can receive conflicting required treatment depending on which unqualified HOLD instruction is implemented.

### Finding M1 — Conditional bundle closure conflicts with the independently accepted context requirement

Material · High priority

Textual anchors

Section 5, Content exposure closure, loading and bounded links, under `MATERIAL_CONTEXT_DEPENDENCY`, requires that necessary original context be added as a “permitted, independently accepted original source” in an atomic author-visible bundle, or that the dependent claim/excerpt be held.&#x20;

Section 7, Context-search and conditional atomic bundle closure, explicitly permits a proposed `CONDITIONAL_BUNDLE` of mutually dependent source proposals. It states that candidacy can be `CONDITIONALLY_CLOSED` before authorization and directs the reviewer to challenge and release the atomic bundle as a unit.&#x20;

NON_ARM_SOURCE_STATUS_REVIEWER_CONTRACT_CANDIDATE_V04.md



Section 3 also permits `CANDIDATE_EXCERPT` where co-release dependencies have been conditionally closed as a proposed atomic bundle.&#x20;

NON_ARM_SOURCE_STATUS_REVIEWER_CONTRACT_CANDIDATE_V04.md



Smallest fictional counterexample

Assume two complete, accessible, contamination-clean original records:

- Original record A contains the exact span: “The blue containers may be used after the evening inspection.”
- Original record B qualifies that rule: “The permission recorded in A excludes containers with broken seals.”

A alone would give the author a materially overbroad interpretation. B alone would lack its governing subject. The two originals have been inspected, their dependency edges identified, and their proposed combined author exposure checked for substantive context. There are no missing components or known contamination blockers.

The proposed author-facing exposure is the exact span from A together with the controlling original context from B, disclosed atomically with neutral metadata.

Neither member has yet undergone independent challenge. No individual or bundled release has been authorized.

Conflicting decision paths

Under Sections 3 and 7, the semantic reviewer can record the appropriate source candidate proposals and a `CONDITIONALLY_CLOSED` dependency against the proposed atomic bundle. Release remains held until independent challenge and authorization.

Under a literal application of Section 5, the required original context is not yet independently accepted. The reviewer must therefore hold the dependent claim/excerpt.

These are not necessarily equivalent outcomes.

One reviewer could assign `CANDIDATE_EXCERPT / PROPOSED` with a conditional context dependency and a separate `RELEASE_HOLD`. Another could assign `HOLD_FOR_SOURCE_REVIEW` to the same fully supported excerpt solely because B has not yet been independently accepted.

If the latter HOLD is semantic, the intended prechallenge atomic-bundle pathway is defeated.

Why the surrounding language does not fully settle it

Section 1 distinguishes candidate status from independent challenge and release authorization. Section 7 clearly attempts to prevent circular dependency deadlock.

However, neither explicitly declares that Section 5's independently accepted context requirement applies at release, rather than at initial source candidacy.

Nor is the ambiguity eliminated by distinguishing acceptance from authorization. The counterexample assumes neither independent acceptance nor release authorization has occurred. Section 7 nonetheless needs to support a proposal before the atomic challenge.

The contract therefore leaves the necessary prechallenge decision underdetermined.

Minimal correction

Amend Section 5 to distinguish proposal closure from release closure:

> At semantic proposal stage, a material original-context dependency may be conditionally closed against an exact, substantively assessed proposed atomic bundle with complete membership and dependency evidence. Independent acceptance is required before author disclosure, not before recording an otherwise supportable candidate proposal. The bundle and dependent exposure remain release-held until the required independent challenge and exact authorization succeed. Missing, prohibited, or materially unqualified context continues to impose the appropriate substantive HOLD or veto.

Define `CONDITIONALLY_CLOSED` explicitly as a dependency-closure attribute, not a seventh content disposition, challenger verdict, or authorization state.

Risk introduced by the correction

Removing the word accepted without substituting substantive proposal requirements could allow an author-relevant exception to be represented by an uninspected placeholder.

That would turn a genuine context deficit into apparent conditional closure and create a false-admission risk.

The correction must therefore retain exact member identification, adequate semantic inspection, all controlling original-context edges, and independent challenge before release.

Finding: A material undefined prechallenge decision exists. The text requires a stage-specific clarification; no new source disposition is necessary.

Smallest fictional counterexample

A complete original note states:

> “On 5 May, the committee proposed using a second verification step.”

The claim is strictly historical: the proposal was recorded on that date. It does not assert adoption, activation, continuing authority, or deployment.

Assume:

- The note is fully delivered and faithfully inspected.
- Its source identity and provenance are adequate for the bounded historical claim.
- All exposed content and metadata have been substantively screened without identifying a material contamination defect.
- The reviewer has constructed a frozen, documented coverage witness that reasonably establishes why the remaining identified but unreviewed records cannot alter this narrow proposition.
- No substantive coverage deficiency has been identified.
- An independent challenger has not yet been assigned.

The proposed author exposure is the complete original note, with an independently controlled release gate.

Conflicting decision paths

Section 1 permits `CANDIDATE_FULL / PROPOSED` because the lack of an external challenge alone is not a semantic defect.

Section 9 likewise permits semantic proposal before the challenger acts.

But Section 7 makes independently challenged limits part of the context-completeness witness and directs that the affected claim/packet remain HOLD if material coverage is unqualified.

If unqualified includes the absence of an independent challenge, the same missing challenger causes a semantic HOLD that Sections 1 and 9 explicitly disallow.

An implementer must decide whether Section 7 means:

1. Substantive evidence failure: The coverage argument is inadequate or leaves a material unexamined possibility, creating semantic HOLD; or
2. Procedural qualification failure: The coverage argument is substantively supportable but has not yet been independently challenged, creating `CHALLENGE_PENDING` and `RELEASE_HOLD`.

V0.4 does not explicitly distinguish those meanings at this point.

There is also a typing problem: Section 7 says an affected claim remains HOLD, although Section 2 defines source-content HOLD for file/span subjects and packet-level `RELEASE_HOLD` separately. It does not identify the corresponding claim-level decision state.

Why other provisions do not fully resolve it

The broader architecture strongly supports the second interpretation when only challenge is absent. Nevertheless, Section 7 makes independent challenge a listed element of the coverage witness and uses an unqualified HOLD instruction.

A conscientious reviewer could recover the intended distinction by reading Sections 1 and 9 together. The issue is that the exact contract does not specify which status must be recorded when a coverage witness is substantively adequate but awaits independent validation.

This is a material ambiguity because it concerns a necessary decision state, not merely preferred wording.

Minimal correction

Separate coverage evidence qualification from independent challenge qualification:

> At semantic proposal stage, a bounded negative-search witness must include sufficient documented source-universe coverage, inspection depth, identified gaps, and a reasoned materiality assessment. Substantively inadequate coverage imposes the appropriate scoped semantic HOLD. Absence of completed independent challenge alone does not alter a supportable semantic candidate proposal. It leaves independent challenge pending and the associated author exposure release-held. Independent acceptance of the coverage witness is mandatory before author release or author-world freeze.

Replace the untyped instruction that an affected “claim/packet remains HOLD” with the applicable subject and gate:

- Material evidentiary deficiency: affected source claim/exposure `HOLD_FOR_SOURCE_REVIEW`, with dependencies recorded.
- Adequate but unchallenged evidence: source `PROPOSED`, challenge pending, exposure `RELEASE_HOLD`.
- Insufficient author-world completeness: packet/world `RELEASE_HOLD` with reason `HOLD_SOURCE_WORLD_INCOMPLETE`.

Risk introduced by the correction

The correction must not reduce negative-search coverage to an unchecked assertion by the primary reviewer.

A weak coverage argument cannot become a valid candidate merely by calling it proposed. The semantic reviewer still needs concrete evidence that no unresolved material gap exists within the stated scope, and the independent challenger must test that evidence before authorization.

Finding: A material ambiguity between substantive insufficiency and uncompleted independent validation permits inappropriate semantic HOLD for an otherwise supportable historical claim.

### 3.1 Relationship between M1 and M2

The findings share a root cause but affect different evidence interfaces.

M1 concerns positive context dependency: when necessary original context exists in a proposed atomic bundle but has not yet been independently accepted.

M2 concerns negative context coverage: when a substantively supported coverage witness exists but its limits have not yet been independently challenged.

The shared missing rule is:

A complete proposed evidence argument must be distinguished from an independently accepted evidence argument, without allowing the former to authorize disclosure.

One stage-precedence amendment could resolve both findings. Two independent new disposition tokens or an extensive redesign are not required.

## 5. Two-sided assessment

### 5.1 False-admission boundary

No reproducible authorized false-admission pathway was established under a complete, consistent application of the written protections.

The contract expressly blocks the strongest invented admission attacks:

| Attack                                                       | Controlling protection                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unsupported source content treated as fully inspected        | Typed representation states and parent dependency HOLD         |
| Confirmed protected excerpt hidden behind missing delivery   | Persistent scoped prohibition and independent delivery blocker |
| Clean excerpt released without necessary context             | Original-source dependency and atomic packet requirements      |
| One-site evidence turned into universal historical authority | Time, activation and applicability-domain requirements         |
| Dynamic content admitted solely by hashing an original file  | Exposure closure and author-view witness                       |
| Safe individual files combined into a spoiler                | Packet-level disclosure screening                              |
| Public totals leaking an excluded member                     | Cumulative join/differencing analysis                          |
| Unqualified reviewer or challenger authorizing material      | Separate role, custody, challenge and authorization gates      |
| Later input change preserving an old release                 | Append-only invalidation and requalification                   |

This is a textual finding only. It does not establish that any future reviewer will successfully identify all protected content, that every rendering pathway can be captured, or that a complete source universe is available.

The false-admission defenses depend on the intended gate separation being implemented correctly. M1 and M2 should therefore be resolved before using V0.4 as an operational decision specification.

### 5.2 False-exclusion boundary

The general false-exclusion design is substantially stronger than a rule that equates every uncertainty with semantic prohibition.

V0.4 already protects otherwise eligible claims against permanent exclusion merely because:

- release authorization or independent challenge is pending;
- later deployment is unknown but a narrower historical claim is established;
- a reference is inert and immaterial;
- a clean source is nonnormative or omitted from a packet;
- a prohibited complete file contains a separately defensible clean excerpt.

However, the false-exclusion boundary is not fully determinate at the two identified context-qualification interfaces.

M1 can turn an otherwise supported conditional bundle proposal into semantic HOLD because its necessary original context has not already been independently accepted.

M2 can turn an adequately evidenced negative-search proposal into semantic HOLD because its coverage limits have not yet been independently challenged.

Both errors arise from prematurely applying an independent-acceptance requirement to semantic candidacy. Neither needs to be solved by weakening the final release requirements.

## 6. Method-text recommendation

## AMEND_MATERIAL_CONTRADICTION

Scope: V0.4 method text only

The V0.4 contract is substantially coherent across its typed subjects, contamination protections, temporal claims, packet construction, public disclosure, and release separation. Nevertheless, M1 and M2 leave materially different compliant interpretations of the required prechallenge semantic status.

The contract should not be treated as a fully resolved operational method specification until these stage-precedence ambiguities are corrected.

### 6.1 Required amendment

The smallest adequate amendment is to add an explicit stage-precedence rule governing both conditional positive context and bounded negative-search coverage.

Suggested contract text:

### Proposal-stage context qualification and release-stage acceptance

A source-content proposal may receive its supported candidate disposition at `PROPOSED` when all substantive claim, original-context, exposure, and bounded-coverage requirements applicable to that proposal have been evidenced, even if independent challenge, release authorization, or custody qualification is not yet complete.

For positive context dependencies, necessary original sources may be represented as an exact proposed `CONDITIONAL_BUNDLE`, provided every member and dependency has been sufficiently identified and substantively assessed. `CONDITIONALLY_CLOSED` is a dependency-closure attribute of the proposal, not a source-content disposition, independent acceptance, or permission for partial or atomic disclosure.

For negative-search coverage, distinguish substantive insufficiency of the coverage witness from the absence of completed independent challenge. A material unresolved coverage gap imposes the appropriate scoped semantic HOLD. An otherwise adequate but not yet independently challenged witness leaves the semantic proposal intact, with challenge pending and the corresponding author exposure release-held.

Required positive context and material negative-search limits must be independently accepted before the corresponding atomic author exposure can be authorized. No conditional candidate, unchallenged coverage witness, or proposed bundle constitutes author admission, final packet sufficiency, or author-world freeze.

Confirmed protected material, missing necessary original context, materially unsupported coverage, unqualified custody, unresolved material challenge, and all other existing release vetoes retain their separate effects. Every disposition, challenge outcome, release gate, and world-sufficiency result must be recorded in its appropriate typed layer.

This amendment reconciles Sections 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 without introducing a new source-content disposition or relaxing the contamination prohibitions.
