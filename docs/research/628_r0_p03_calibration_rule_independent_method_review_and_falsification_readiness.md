## 3. Two practical ambiguities addressed prospectively, without rewriting the rule

1. **Intended author audience and permissible claim.** The rule requires this to be fixed, but the standalone document is reusable across multiple future audiences and periods. The challenge should consistently evaluate whether a truthfully bounded historical/current claim could be safely disclosed to a **fresh blind primary information-need author** for the historical source-world experiment, not alternate arbitrarily between privileged private reviewers or scorers. This matters because a source may be usable for historical truth and still unsafe for public author exposure.
2. **Materiality and evidence-closure limit.** Terms such as `material`, `sufficient` and `credible` are necessarily context-sensitive, but must not allow a reviewer either to pass uninspected historical claims or to HOLD every source indefinitely because *something elsewhere might exist*. A material missing discriminator is a named, plausible original, contradiction, representation or exposure interaction that could change the **particular supported claim, temporal scope or author-release decision**. The reviewer must also attempt a narrower honest claim or independently checked sanitized representation before asserting no path to qualification.

A **source-free, proposed companion** at `experiments/r0_p03_navigation_probe/R0_P03_SOURCE_FAMILY_FALSIFICATION_INTERPRETATION_V01.md` fixes these interpretations, the double-sided challenge, no global source-admission inference, distinct reviewer role and safe-only output requirements. It does **not** change the exact original rule or imply endorsement of any real case.

Its own precise bytes/hash must be checked and explicitly frozen along with the original rule *before* anyone else sees the heldout ZIP. If the interpretation were later to be substantively changed based on holdout discoveries, it would be a new prospective version and not a retroactive successful falsification.
